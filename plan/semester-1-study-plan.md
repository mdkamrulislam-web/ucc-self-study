Sixty hours is a lot, but it is spread over long library days with lunch at home, a break before dinner and a free Saturday evening. From 26 October, when the backlog is gone, the Sunday buffer and Friday afternoon are the first blocks to give back if you are worn out.

Sleep is 6 hours, one more than you allowed, and that hour costs no study. The library opens at 07:30 and you get sleepy studying at home, so the time before 05:30 was never going to be study time. The extra hour also helps memory, because a day's study is consolidated while you sleep.

## Rules that keep it on track

A plan is bullet-proof when it says what to drop on a bad day, not when every hour is filled.

1. **48-hour rule.** Study each lecture's guide section within 48 hours of the lecture. No module may fall more than one lecture behind.
2. **Hard modules get the mornings.** EE6041 and EE6049 own the first library block of the day. It is the last thing you give up.
3. **Drop in this order.** When a day goes wrong, drop Python first, then EE6043, then the late block. Never the deep block, and never a lab deadline.
4. **Finish early.** Submit CS6322 labs by Sunday 15:00, not 23:59, and do lab pre-reading the evening before.
5. **Sunday review, 1 hour.** Tick your progress on [study.whoiskamrul.com](https://study.whoiskamrul.com/), count lectures behind per module, and move the Sunday buffer to whichever is furthest behind.
6. **Saturday evening is off.** Rest is part of the plan; without it the week collapses by November.
7. **Lab Thursdays.** On 8, 22 and 29 Oct you are in the lab until 19:00, so eat your packed dinner after the lab and do the worksheet from 19:30 to 21:30.
8. **CS6322 labs are yours.** The lecturer asks that GenAI is not used for lab answers, so use the guide for ideas and do the lab work yourself.
9. **Exam mode from 16 Nov.** The late blocks switch to past papers, one module per night, hardest two twice a week.
